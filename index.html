P41 extended 2h → 6h: what actually changed

Time from task notification to the CSP's first required action. The post-change cohort is scoped to bookings where the customer proposed the slot, so technician assignment genuinely is the CSP's first step. The extra four hours did not make CSPs faster — it stopped killing the tasks they were always going to get to.

The one-line answer

Extending P41 delivers about +5.5pp more tasks actioned, and it does so entirely in the window the old config used to discard — between working-hour 2 and working-hour 6. Under the 2h window the curve stopped dead at hour 2 with tasks still arriving; under 6h it keeps accruing and only flattens at hour 6. CSP responsiveness in the first two hours is not what changed — what changed is how much of their work survived long enough to count.

Do not read the vertical gap as the P41 effect

POST sits ~7pp above PRE from the very first quarter-hour — before either deadline could possibly matter. That gap is cohort composition, not P41. The POST cohort is filtered to customers who actively proposed a slot at booking, which is a higher-intent population; PRE has no equivalent subset because the customer-scheduling flow did not exist yet. Applying the one filter component that is symmetric — valid Indian mobile — moves PRE by essentially nothing (4,985 → 4,903 candidates, action rate 33.1% → 33.1%), confirming the gap comes from the proposedBy component alone.

The P41 effect is the within-cohort accrual after hour 2: +5.52pp on this cohort, and +4.96pp on the unfiltered post-change cohort. Consistent at roughly +5pp either way, which is what makes it trustworthy.

The change is real in the config

Verified rather than assumed: the configured minimum P41_DEADLINE_AT − CREATED_AT steps from exactly 2.0h to exactly 6.0h on 22 July. But the effective window is far longer than 6h for most tasks, because the deadline rolls to the next working day when it would otherwise expire after hours.

POST's higher percentiles are not "CSPs got slower"

PRE's distribution is right-truncated by its own deadline. Under a 2h window you cannot observe a five-hour slot proposal — the task was already dead. PRE's flattering P95 of 1.68 working hours is therefore largely an artifact of the guillotine, not evidence of faster CSPs. POST's P95 of 4.41 working hours is a fuller measurement, not a worse one. The censoring-consistent comparison is the curve shape and the action rate, never the conditional percentiles.

The remaining caveat

45% of post-change assignments are not a CSP acting

433 of 960 assignments complete within 60 seconds of candidate creation. Tracing them: the DAS allocation, candidate creation, SLOT_CONFIRMED_BY_CUSTOMER and AWAITING_TECHNICIAN_ASSIGNMENT all land within ~2 seconds of each other, then TECHNICIAN_ASSIGNED fires 6–52 seconds later.

The actors are real, varying CSP account IDs and the downstream outcomes are genuine (CONNECTION_ACTIVE, CANCELLED_BY_CUSTOMER, INSTALLATION_REPORTED_FAILED) — so this is not test data. But no human assigns a technician in 30 seconds. The sequence is being replayed into the new stack rather than performed on it. The mechanism could not be determined from these tables alone; it needs an engineering answer. A comparable cluster exists in PRE (728 of 4,903), so the cohort comparison stays fair — but any percentile over the unfiltered set is meaningless, which is why the excl.-replay rows exist.

Methodology

Cohort definitions
  • Cohort anchor — task/candidate creation date (CREATED_AT, IST), tracked forward to whenever the action occurred. Not event-date. One row per EXECUTION_CANDIDATE_ID.
  • POST = candidates created 26 Jul – 1 Aug 2026 on customer-proposed-slot bookings → 2,083 candidates (of 3,850 created that week). 99.2% of them independently carry SLOT_CONFIRMATION_SOURCE='CUSTOMER_PROPOSED' on the candidate row, which cross-validates the filter.
  • PRE = candidates created 14–20 Jul 2026 with a valid Indian mobile → 4,903 candidates. The proposedBy condition has no PRE equivalent: the customer-scheduling flow launched ~21 Jul.
  • Clock start ("notif") — CREATED_AT, the moment the task is pushed to the CSP, and the exact anchor P41 is measured from.
  • Clock end, POST — first TO_STATE='TECHNICIAN_ASSIGNED', excluding TECHNICIAN_ASSIGNED → TECHNICIAN_ASSIGNED reassignments. Because the customer proposed the slot, this is the CSP's first required action.
  • Clock end, PRE — first CSP-actor transition to SLOT_SELECTED / AWAITING_CUSTOMER_SLOT_CONFIRMATION. In the pre-change flow, proposing a slot was the CSP's first required step, so this is the correct like-for-like action. Both states are written at the same instant; taking the earlier avoids a tie-break bias.
  • Declines excluded from "actioned" — a decline is a response but not progress. Reported separately.
  • Right-censoring — none material: the POST curve is flat from hour 6 to hour 48, and the 1 Aug cohort had ≥48h observed as of 3 Aug.
POST cohort filter (as specified) 
SELECT slot_details, mobile, name, payment_status, status, account_id
FROM prod_db.dynamodb_read.booking
WHERE slot_details IS NOT NULL
  AND PARSE_JSON(slot_details):proposedBy::STRING = 'CUSTOMER'
  AND ( mobile LIKE '6%' OR mobile LIKE '7%'
     OR mobile LIKE '8%' OR mobile LIKE '9%' );

Bridged to candidates via COMPANY_B_CONNECTION_BOOKING_ENRICHED on MOBILE → CONNECTION_ID, with _FIVETRAN_DELETED = FALSE added.

Known limitation: dynamodb_read.booking is a current-state snapshot keyed on mobile, not booking history. If a customer rebooked after the window, slot_details reflects the later booking. This is the most likely reason 1,098 candidates carrying SLOT_CONFIRMATION_SOURCE='CUSTOMER_PROPOSED' on the candidate row fall outside the filter. The filter is therefore conservative — it under-selects rather than contaminating.

Working-hours calculation

Working hours = 9 AM – 9 PM IST, 12h/day, counted on all seven days (field installs run weekends). Computed as a monotonic working-time coordinate rather than a day-by-day loop:

WT(t) = day_index(t) × 43200
      + clamp( seconds_since_0900(t), 0, 43200 )

working_seconds = WT(end) − WT(start)

Validated against worked examples — created 30 Jul 16:06 → assigned 31 Jul 12:18 is 20.2 calendar hours, of which 4.9h (16:06–21:00) + 3.3h (09:00–12:18) = 8.2 working hours. ✓

Timezone handling verified independently: CONVERT_TIMEZONE('Asia/Kolkata', …) and +330 minutes agree on every row, confirming the stored offset is UTC. The resulting IST creation distribution peaks at 3 PM and troughs at 3–5 AM, consistent with Indian consumer booking behaviour.

Open questions for engineering
  • What produces the sub-60-second assignment cluster? 433 post-change and 728 pre-change candidates have their entire chain replayed at ingest. Until this is explained, every latency metric on this funnel needs it filtered out.
  • Should P41 be stated in working hours? The deadline already rolls over for after-hours expiry — which is why the effective median window is ~16 calendar hours rather than 6, and why the observed cap sits at exactly 6 working hours. Expressing the parameter in working hours would make the config match its behaviour.
  • Is a further extension worth it? Probably not. The curve is flat from hour 6 to hour 48 — zero assignments arrive late. The 713 remaining TIMEOUT_P41 deaths are not CSPs who ran out of time; they are CSPs who never engaged. That is a routing or supply problem, not a timer problem.
