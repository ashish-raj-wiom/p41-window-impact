P41 extended 2h → 6h: what actually changed

Time from task notification to the CSP's first required action, for install candidates created 26 Jul – 1 Aug 2026 versus the pre-change week. The post-change cohort is customer-proposed-slot bookings; the action measured on them is technician assignment. The extra four hours did not make CSPs faster — it stopped killing the tasks they were always going to get to.

The one-line answer

Under the 2-hour window PRE stopped dead at hour 2 with tasks still arriving. Under 6 hours POST keeps accruing for another four — +4.61pp — then flattens absolutely at hour 6, with nothing whatsoever beyond it. That within-cohort accrual is the P41 effect, and it doesn't depend on who is in which cohort. CSP responsiveness didn't improve; what improved is how much of their work survived long enough to count.

But only just over half of what the extra hours surface is progress. Total CSP response rises +8.50pp between hour 2 and hour 6 — of which +4.61pp is a technician assignment and +3.89pp is a decline. See the response split.

Read the shape of each curve, never the gap between them

The two cohorts are deliberately scoped differently: POST is customer-proposed-slot bookings, PRE is the whole pre-change week, because that flow did not exist before ~21 Jul and so has no equivalent subset. POST therefore sits above PRE from the very first quarter-hour — before either deadline can bind — and that vertical gap is cohort composition, not P41. Only the accrual within each curve after hour 2 is attributable to the change. See the scoping notes below.

The decline half is not free — it delays rerouting

Under the 2-hour window, a CSP who was going to refuse simply timed out at hour 2 and the booking was released for reroute. Under six hours, that same CSP now holds the booking and declines at hour 3, 4 or 5 — so the customer waits up to four extra working hours before the task even starts looking for someone else. The same applies to the 35.5% who still time out: they now hold the booking for six hours instead of two.

So the honest ledger is +4.61pp genuinely converted to progress, bought at the cost of delayed release on the +3.89pp who decline late plus the whole timeout population. Whether that trade is worth it depends on how quickly a rerouted booking finds a second CSP — which this analysis does not measure.

POST's higher percentiles are not "CSPs got slower"

PRE's distribution is right-truncated by its own deadline. Under a 2h window you cannot observe a five-hour slot proposal — the task was already dead. PRE's flattering P95 of 1.44 working hours is therefore largely an artifact of the guillotine, not evidence of faster CSPs. POST's P95 of 5.35 working hours is a fuller measurement, not a worse one. The censoring-consistent comparison is the curve shape and the action rate, never the conditional percentiles.

Scoping notes — worth knowing before reusing this

1 · proposedBy is a flow marker, not a customer attribute

It is tempting to scope the post-change cohort with slot_details:proposedBy = 'CUSTOMER'. But that field takes exactly one value across the entire booking table — CUSTOMER, on 2,388 rows out of 1,104,543; every other booking has slot_details NULL. So it does not distinguish customers who proposed a slot from those who didn't; it simply flags membership of the post-21-Jul customer-scheduling flow.

Applied to both weeks it matches 53.1% of POST candidates but only 2.1% of PRE. Using it on one side of a comparison silently replaces the denominator with "tasks that arrive with a slot already confirmed" — exactly the population that can be assigned within seconds — which lifts the whole curve by ~7pp at every elapsed time and invents an improvement that isn't there.

2 · dynamodb_read.booking is a mutable snapshot

It holds current state keyed on mobile, not booking history, so figures derived from it are not reproducible. The identical query run twice ~40 minutes apart:

First run40 min later
Candidates matched2,0832,045
Assigned960956
Died TIMEOUT_P41713689

Use SLOT_CONFIRMATION_SOURCE = 'CUSTOMER_PROPOSED' on the candidate row instead. Same intent, but it sits on the immutable candidate record so it doesn't drift, and it's more complete — 3,165 candidates versus 2,045, because it doesn't lose rows to snapshot overwrites. The two agree on what they select (the booking filter cross-validated at 99.2%); one is just stable and the other isn't. Every customer-proposed figure on this page uses the candidate-side field.

Methodology

Cohort definitions
  • Cohort anchor — task/candidate creation date (CREATED_AT, IST), tracked forward to whenever the action occurred. Not event-date. One row per EXECUTION_CANDIDATE_ID.
  • PRE = all candidates created 14–20 Jul 2026 → 4,985. POST = all candidates created 26 Jul – 1 Aug 2026 whose booking has a customer-proposed slot → 3,165 (of 3,850 created that week). PRE is not subset: the customer-scheduling flow launched ~21 Jul, so it has no equivalent population.
  • Clock start ("notif") — CREATED_AT, the moment the task is pushed to the CSP, and the exact anchor P41 is measured from.
  • Clock end, PRE — first CSP-actor transition to SLOT_SELECTED / AWAITING_CUSTOMER_SLOT_CONFIRMATION. In the pre-change flow, proposing a slot was the CSP's first required step. Both states are written at the same instant; taking the earlier avoids a tie-break bias.
  • Clock end, POST — first TO_STATE='TECHNICIAN_ASSIGNED', excluding TECHNICIAN_ASSIGNED → TECHNICIAN_ASSIGNED reassignments. The customer-scheduling flow launched ~21 Jul, so for most of this cohort the slot arrives already confirmed and assigning a technician is the CSP's first required step.
  • Why the actions differ — each cohort measures the CSP's first required action under the flow in force that week. Holding the action name fixed across the change would compare a step that was mandatory in one regime against one that barely existed in the other.
  • Declines excluded from "actioned" — a decline is a response but not progress. Reported separately.
  • Right-censoring — not material: the POST curve is flat from hour 8 onward, and the 1 Aug cohort had ≥48h observed.
Working-hours calculation

Working hours = 9 AM – 9 PM IST, 12h/day, counted on all seven days (field installs run weekends). Computed as a monotonic working-time coordinate rather than a day-by-day loop:

WT(t) = day_index(t) × 43200
      + clamp( seconds_since_0900(t), 0, 43200 )

working_seconds = WT(end) − WT(start)

Validated against worked examples — a task created 30 Jul 16:06 and assigned 31 Jul 12:18 accrues 4.9h (16:06–21:00) on day one plus 3.3h (09:00–12:18) on day two = 8.2 working hours. ✓

Timezone handling verified three ways — DATEADD(minute,330,…) on the raw TIMESTAMP_TZ, the same after TO_TIMESTAMP_NTZ, and CONVERT_TIMEZONE('Asia/Kolkata',…) — all agree exactly (3,850 candidates in the POST window under each). The resulting IST creation distribution peaks at 3 PM and troughs at 3–5 AM, consistent with Indian consumer booking behaviour.

Open questions for engineering
  • P41 is already a working-hours timer — is that documented anywhere? Measured on that clock the window is exactly 2.00 then 6.00, with min, median and max identical. The behaviour is correct and deliberate; the risk is that anyone reading the parameter as "6 hours" and checking it against a wall clock will conclude it is misfiring. Worth stating the unit explicitly wherever P41 is configured or monitored.
  • Is a further extension worth it? Probably not. The curve is flat from hour 8 to hour 48. The 1,441 remaining TIMEOUT_P41 deaths are not CSPs who ran out of time; they are CSPs who never engaged. That is a routing or supply problem, not a timer problem.
  • Is P41 doing anything in the non-customer-proposed flow? There, technician assignment happens after customer slot confirmation and is unbounded — the whole >6h tail sits in that segment. Worth confirming which stage P41 is intended to police once the customer-scheduling flow is the default.
