SD questions

Questions raised on the P41 analysis, and what the data says to each.

← The analysis SD questions

Each answer opens with the conclusion, then the data behind it. Every figure here is counted from the record — response times, acceptances, installs and re-offers as they actually happened. Where a question cannot be answered from observation, that is said plainly rather than modelled around.

SD Q1. What is total system efficiency of install for P41 at 2 hour, 4 hour and 6 hour? Total efficiency is different from one CSP efficiency.

Estimating it is tricky, and the honest answer has two halves.

What is certain is the leading metric. Response rate rises from 54% at 2 hours to 59% at 4 and 62% at 6. Acceptance rises with it. And an acceptance that arrives late converts to an install just as well as one that arrives immediately — around 50% either way. So the extra hours buy real, equal-quality work.

What is not knowable from observation is the offsetting half: how many installs stop happening at a higher P41 that were happening at a lower one, because the booking used to be retriggered to another CSP sooner. That cannot be settled by looking at history — it needs an experiment. Run P41 at different values across cohorts for a week and measure it.

The leading metric — certain

Every offer is recorded with the exact time the CSP answered. Counting how many landed inside 2, 4 or 6 hours needs no assumption at all:

A late acceptance is worth as much as an early one

If the later hours were only picking up reluctant CSPs who then fail to deliver, the extra response would be worthless. They are not:

The unknown, and why history cannot settle it

A booking whose CSP never answers is not lost. It goes to someone else, and it often still gets done: 543 bookings (33%) got no answer from their first CSP, and 118 of them installed anyway — 21.7%, through a retrigger to another CSP.

Raise P41 and those retriggers start later. Some of those 118 installs would not happen. How many is the entire question, and it is not visible in the data: the window is 6 hours, so we have never observed a retrigger that began at hour 8, nor a CSP given the chance to answer in hour 7.

Recommendation: A/B test the parameter. Set P41 to different values across comparable cohorts, run for a week, and read total install rate directly. That measures the two halves together — the response gained and the retriggers lost — without needing to assume anything about either.

SD Q2. A CSP gets the same lead twice — what is the impact of that?

It depends how the lead reached him — and neither case is worth much.

If he declined it the first time and it comes back, he ignores or declines it again. Effectively 0% install.

If it timed out the first time and comes back, there is a small chance he acts. Around 3–6% install.

Behaviour repeats itself. A CSP who ignored the booking ignores it again four times in five. One who declined it declines again at the same rate — and that group returned a single install from 170 attempts. Re-asking a CSP who has already said no is as close to worthless as this data contains.

The cost is real, because each of those offers holds the booking for a full window before it can move on. One booking in four is re-offered to a CSP who has already seen it, and for those bookings 46% of every offer made is a repeat.

The implication is a routing rule, not a timer value: any P41 setting goes further if a CSP who has already timed out or declined is not asked again while an unasked CSP is available.

Already in flight — this ships this week. Suppressing the re-offer to a CSP who has declined is an item already planned to go live, so the 170 cases above and the windows they consume should stop appearing.

It also makes the P41 question cleaner: once those windows are freed, the runway is being spent on CSPs who have not yet seen the booking, and any measurement of the parameter is no longer confounded by re-asking people who have already refused.

SD Q3. Why is P95 not the right cut-off, given the lead can go to someone else and/or come back again?

Because P95 is set by the cut-off, not by the CSP. Using it to choose the cut-off is circular.

More time does produce more response — that much the leading metric shows. But P95 is around 4 hours only because the window is 6. Raise the window and P95 rises with it. It can justify any number it is pointed at.

The guardrail should come from the customer instead: cancellation spikes the day after the confirmed slot, which works back to about 8 working hours per CSP. That is the recommendation.

P95 moves with the window; the median does not

The window has been at 2 hours and at 6. The response distribution under each:

The window tripled and P95 went up 2.6×. Meanwhile P50 did not move at all — about a minute in both regimes. The median is a real property of how CSPs behave. The tail is a property of how long we let them take.

So "cover P95" has no fixed point. At a 2-hour window it argues for 1h 34m. At 6 it argues for 4h 06m. At 8 it would argue for something near 6. The measure is downstream of the decision it is meant to inform.

The two rows are each regime's first required CSP action — proposing a slot before the flow change, assigning a technician after — so the comparison is not perfectly like-for-like. The direction and rough magnitude are not in doubt.

And the lead is not lost when the window closes

A CSP who does not answer is not the end of the booking. It goes to someone else, and 21.7% of those bookings still install. So the last few percent of the response tail are being bought at the cost of delaying the retrigger for everyone who was never going to answer — which is precisely the trade P95 is blind to.

What should set the cut-off

The customer's own deadline. Cancellation is near-zero until the promised slot day and then steps up sharply, peaking the afternoon after it. Working back from that point the median booking has 15.5 working hours before the deadline and passes through 1.9 CSPs — about 8 working hours each.

That guardrail is derived from customer behaviour, not from the parameter's own past values. It cannot be moved by changing the setting, which is exactly what P95 can.
