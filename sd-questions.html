SD Q1 supersedes the recommendation in the main analysis. It simulates total system efficiency and finds 6 working hours is the optimum and 8 is worse. SD Q1. What is total system efficiency of install for P41 at 2 hour, 4 hour and 6 hour? Total efficiency is different from one CSP efficiency.

39.4%, 40.7% and 41.5%. And at 8 hours it falls back to 41.0%.

Total system efficiency is not one CSP's efficiency, and the difference is the whole answer. Getting it needs a simulation, because the reroute is not a fixed fallback: a shorter window sends the booking to the next CSP earlier, with more runway left, and the rescue rate depends on exactly that.

Read the 8-hour row across. Response rate and positive response rate are the best in the table — the individual CSP keeps improving with more time. But bookings served before the deadline fall, 60.9% to 59.9%, and overall install falls with them. At 8 hours every failed attempt burns 8 working hours of runway instead of 6, so bookings needing a second CSP run past the promised day: past-deadline failures rise from 205 to 266. More answers per offer, fewer bookings served.

How it is built. Each booking's actual CSP chain is replayed under a candidate P41. Every CSP's own response — accept, decline or silence — and his own latency are taken as observed; the only counterfactual is where the window truncates. Requeue lag is set to zero because that is what it measures: after a decline the next offer goes out in a median of 0.0 hours, after a timeout in 14.6 calendar hours, which is the timer itself. Population is the 1,030 bookings carrying a confirmed slot date.

Three checks it passes. At 6 hours it reproduces observed reality to 0.1 points — 60.9% served against 60.8% actual. The optimum stays at 6 hours under all three deadline definitions (end of the slot day, noon the day after, end of the day after). And the truncation assumption holds: response curves inside the first two hours are near-parallel between the 2-hour and 6-hour regimes, so CSPs are not pacing themselves to the deadline.

The limitation, and which way it cuts. The simulation can only try CSPs who were actually offered the booking. Under a shorter window more reroutes would fit and we do not know who they would be — 296 bookings run out of chain at 2 hours against 198 at 6. Crediting one extra attempt at the observed 41.6% acceptance rate moves the optimum to 3 hours (45.6%). So the optimum is 6 hours if the reroute pool is shallow and 3 hours if it is deep — but 8 hours is worse than both in every specification run.
